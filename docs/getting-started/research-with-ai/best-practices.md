# Best Practices

You now have a powerful set of tools and a workflow for using them effectively. Before you begin applying them to your coursework, let's recap the most important best practices and discuss the ethical guidelines for using AI in an academic setting.


## Common Best Practices ✅

Everything we've discussed comes down to a few core principles. Keep these in mind every time you use an AI assistant:

* **Provide Specificity and Context**: Remember the "Four Pillars." The quality of your output is directly tied to the quality of your input. Always provide a clear role, task, context, and format to guide the AI toward a useful response.
* **Iterate and Refine**: Treat your interaction as a conversation, not a one-time transaction. Use the AI's first response as a starting point to ask deeper, more specific follow-up questions.
* **Maintain Critical Evaluation**: This is the most important skill. Never blindly trust or copy-paste AI output. Always treat it as a first draft generated by a brilliant but sometimes flawed assistant. Your job is to verify its claims, question its logic, add your unique insights, and make the final judgment.


## Academic Integrity 🎓

Using AI tools in this course is not only allowed; it's encouraged. However, it must be done in a way that supports your learning and adheres to the principles of academic honesty. Using these tools improperly is a serious academic offense.

* **Review the Official Course AI Policy**: This is mandatory. The definitive rules for using and citing AI in your graded work are located in the course syllabus [here](../../syllabus/ai-policy.md). You are responsible for knowing and following this policy.
* **When and How to Cite AI**: The core principle is transparency. If you incorporate a specific idea, a unique phrase, a piece of data, or a structural element that was generated by an AI into your final submitted work, you must cite it. The official AI policy provides the exact citation format to use.

Think of it this way:

| **Appropriate Use (Process) 👍** | **Inappropriate Use (Product) 👎** |
| :--- | :--- |
| Brainstorming ideas for your project | Submitting an essay written by the AI |
| Explaining a complex concept to you like a tutor | Copy-pasting AI-generated text into your report |
| Summarizing your research notes from NotebookLM | Using an AI summary as the final analysis section |
| Generating a first draft to help you overcome writer's block | Turning in that first draft without significant rewriting and editing |

The goal of this course is for **you** to develop critical thinking and analytical skills. AI is a powerful tool to assist you in that process, but it is not a substitute for it.


## A Final Thought 

The graded projects in this course are designed for a specific reason: to give voice to your unique ideas and lived experiences through the prism of business and management theory. This isn't just an academic exercise; in my opinion, it is the very essence of effective business, management, and leadership.

In a world where AI can generate generic answers, your unique perspective, critical judgment, and creative insights are what make you valuable. If you decide that AI has the final word on your assignments, you are not just completing a task—you are practicing the habit of relegating your own voice.

Your goal here isn't just to pass a course; it's to build a compelling case for your own relevance. Your future employers are not looking to hire someone who can operate a chatbot. They are looking to hire **you**—your mind, your experience, and your voice. Use the tools we've discussed to sharpen your thinking and amplify that voice, not replace it.
