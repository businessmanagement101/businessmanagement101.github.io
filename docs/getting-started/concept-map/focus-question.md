# Focus Question Strategies

## **Finding Your North Star: Four Strategies for a Great Focus Question** ⭐

Your **conceptual focus question** is the most important part of this assignment. It's the "North Star" that guides the construction of your map and the entire argument of your written narrative. A strong question is **analytical** (asking "how" or "why"), not **descriptive** (asking "what"), and it focuses on the relationship between *concepts*, not just the actions of a company.

Finding the perfect question can be challenging, but it's a creative process. Here are four strategies to help you brainstorm. Try the one that best fits your style of thinking.

### **1. Find the Friction** 🔥

Business is full of natural tensions and trade-offs. These conflicts are a goldmine for insightful questions because they force you to analyze how opposing forces are managed.

* **How it works:** Look for concepts from the course that seem to pull in opposite directions and ask a question about how they interact.
* **Look for tensions like:**
    * Short-Term Profit vs. Long-Term Brand Building
    * Standardization & Efficiency vs. Customization & Innovation
    * Shareholder Interests vs. Stakeholder (Employee, Customer, Society) Interests
    * Disruptive Innovation vs. Maintaining an Existing Business Model

### **2. The Concept Mash-up** 🧩

This is a structured brainstorming method to spark creative connections between different areas of the course.

* **How it works:**
    1.  Create two columns on a piece of paper. In Column A, list 5-10 "big picture" strategy concepts (e.g., Competitive Advantage, Corporate Culture).
    2.  In Column B, list 5-10 more specific functional concepts (e.g., Supply Chain, Brand Equity, Team Composition).
    3.  Draw a line connecting one concept from Column A to one in Column B and form a question about their relationship.
* **Example:** You connect **`Corporate Culture`** (Column A) to **`Supply Chain Efficiency`** (Column B). Your question becomes: "*How can a company's internal **Corporate Culture** be a primary driver of its **Supply Chain Efficiency**?*"

### **3. The Placeholder Method** 🏗️

This is a "bottom-up" approach for those who prefer to think by exploring and organizing first. It reduces the pressure to get the question perfect from the start.

* **How it works:**
    1.  Start with a broad, simple placeholder question (e.g., "*I want to explore the relationship between marketing and operations.*").
    2.  Create your concept "parking lot," listing all the relevant ideas you can think of for that general topic.
    3.  Group your concepts into branches and start building a rough draft of your map.
    4.  **Then, look at the connections you've made.** A more specific and interesting question will emerge. You can then refine your simple placeholder into a sharp, analytical question.

### **4. Brainstorm with AI** 🤖

AI assistants can be powerful partners for overcoming the "blank page" problem and generating initial ideas.

* **How it works:** Use a detailed prompt to ask an AI for help. Don't just ask for "a question"; give it the raw materials from the course.
* **Sample Prompt:**
    > "I'm in an intro to business course. I need to write a conceptual focus question about the relationship between two or more of the following concepts: [list 5-7 concepts from class, e.g., disruptive innovation, brand equity, organizational culture, shareholder value, supply chain]. Please generate 5 potential questions for me."
* **Crucial Warning:** Use the AI's output as a **starting point**, not a final answer. The best questions will come from you refining the AI's suggestions to better fit the specific themes and arguments from our course.
