## **:material-numeric-1-box-multiple: The Big Picture**

The goal of this assignment is not to write a report about a company; it is to build and explain **your own integrated theory of management**.

Think of yourself as a theorist. Your primary subject of analysis is the **network of course concepts themselves**—how they fit together, how they influence one another, and how they form a coherent system. You will create a **Concept Map** to visualize these relationships and a **Theoretical Narrative** to explain the logic and implications of your model.

A company of your choice will serve as your running "case study," providing concrete examples that bring your theoretical arguments to life. But remember, the star of this show is the network of ideas, not the company.

## **:material-numeric-2-box-multiple: The Heart of the Project**

Every strong theory begins with a powerful question. The most important step in this assignment is to develop a **conceptual focus question** that will guide your thinking. This question must be about the relationship between *ideas*, not just facts about a company.

To understand the difference, study these examples:

| Type of Question | What it Asks | Good for... |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Empirical Question** (Avoid for this assignment) | "What is Apple's strategy?" | Your Final Company Analysis Project |
| **Conceptual Question** (Pursue for this assignment) | "How does the strategic concept of 'Competitive Advantage' depend on the effective integration of principles from Marketing (e.g., 'Brand Equity') and Operations (e.g., 'Supply Chain Efficiency')?" | **This Assignment** |

**How to Develop a Strong Conceptual Question:**

1.  **Start with a Core Concept:** Pick a major idea from the course that you find interesting (e.g., "Organizational Culture," "Shareholder Primacy," "Disruptive Innovation").
2.  **Connect it to Other Concepts:** Ask yourself, "What other ideas does this depend on? What does this idea influence?"
3.  **Frame it as a "How" or "Why" Question:**
    * "How does a firm's **'Management Ideology'** create predictable tensions with the marketing goal of building long-term **'Brand Equity'**?"
    * "In what ways do the principles of **'Organizational Culture'** serve as the necessary foundation for executing a strategy of **'Disruptive Innovation'**?"
    * "Why must an effective **'Operations Strategy'** be directly informed by a company's financial model of **'Growth vs. Profitability'**?"

Your question will be the central organizing principle for both your map and your narrative.

## **:material-numeric-3-box-multiple: The Visual Blueprint**

The concept map is your visual thinking space. It is where you will organize the course concepts into a hierarchy and draw the cross-links that answer your focus question.

**Step-by-Step Process:**

1.  **Place Your Focus Question at the Top:** This orients you and your reader immediately.
2.  **Identify Your "Tier 1" Concepts:** These are the 2-4 major concepts that are central to your question. Arrange them prominently on the map.
3.  **Build Out the Hierarchy ("Tier 2" and "Tier 3"):** Under each main concept, add related sub-concepts. For example, under "Marketing Strategy," you might have "Brand Equity," "4 P's," and "Market Segmentation." This shows the hierarchical relationship.
4.  **Draw and Label Cross-Links:** This is the most critical step. Draw lines connecting concepts from different hierarchies. On each line, you **must write a short phrase that explains the relationship**. This is where your analysis becomes visible.
    * A line connecting "Supply Chain Efficiency" to "Brand Equity" might be labeled: *"can undermine by sourcing low-quality materials."*
    * A line connecting "Organizational Culture" to "Competitive Advantage" might be labeled: *"creates a difficult-to-imitate source of."*
5.  **Add Illustrative Company Examples:** Sparingly, add very brief examples from your chosen company to ground your conceptual points. A box coming off "Disruptive Innovation" might say: *"e.g., Netflix's shift from DVDs to streaming."* Keep these brief—they are illustrations, not the main point.

## **:material-numeric-4-box-multiple: Theoretical Narrative**

Your narrative is a formal essay that answers your conceptual focus question. It should follow the logic of your map and explain your theoretical argument in detail.

**Key Components of an "A-Level" Narrative:**

1.  **A Clear Introduction and Thesis:** Start by stating your conceptual focus question. Your thesis statement should be a direct and concise answer to that question.
    * **Example Thesis:** *"This essay argues that sustainable 'Competitive Advantage' is not just a strategic choice, but an emergent property that arises when a firm's 'Marketing Strategy' and its 'Operations Strategy' are in perfect alignment. When they are misaligned, one function will inevitably undermine the other, leading to strategic failure."*
2.  **Focus on Analyzing Concepts:** The body of your paper should be organized around the conceptual claims you are making. Use your map as your outline. Each paragraph should explain a key connection you've identified.
3.  **Use Your Company as Evidence, Not the Subject:** This is the most important distinction. Your claims should be about the theories. Use the company to provide vivid, real-world proof of your theoretical points. Study this contrast:

    | Instead of This (Subject is the Company) | Try This (Subject is the Theory) |
    | :--- | :--- |
    | "Tesla has a powerful brand. Its operations are also very innovative. This combination gives it a competitive advantage." | "The concept of **'Brand Equity'** is often viewed as a purely marketing function, but this view is incomplete. A brand's promise is only sustainable if the firm's **'Operational Capabilities'** can consistently deliver on it. For instance, Tesla's brand promises cutting-edge innovation. This promise would be worthless if its operations were incapable of producing high-performance electric vehicles at scale. This illustrates how brand and operations are not separate pillars, but a tightly woven fabric." |
4.  **A Strong Conclusion:** Summarize the answer to your focus question. What are the broader implications of your theory? Why is it important for a manager to understand the connections you've outlined?

## **:material-numeric-5-box-multiple: Final Checklist**

* Is my focus question clearly stated and **conceptual** in nature?
* Does my concept map show both **hierarchy** and **labeled cross-links**?
* Does my narrative have a clear **thesis** that answers my focus question?
* Is the main subject of my narrative the **relationship between concepts**?
* Do I use my company examples **briefly and effectively** to illustrate my theoretical points?
* Have I proofread both the map and the narrative for clarity and professionalism?
