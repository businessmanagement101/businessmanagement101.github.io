## **:material-numeric-1-box-multiple: The Big Picture**

The goal of this paper is not to write a report on what a company does, but to write a persuasive analytical essay that explains **why** a company behaves the way it does. Think of yourself as an analyst whose job is to look past the headlines and marketing to uncover the deep-seated principles that drive a company's strategic decisions.

Your entire paper will be built around a single, powerful argument about the company's core **management ideology**.

## **:material-numeric-2-box-multiple:  Your Thesis**

The heart of your paper is your thesis statement. You will argue that your company's actions are best explained by one of two competing ideologies:

1.  **Shareholder Primacy:** The belief that a company's primary, and perhaps only, duty is to maximize wealth for its owners (the shareholders).
2.  **Stakeholder Primacy:** The belief that a company must balance the needs of its shareholders with the needs of other critical groups, such as its employees, customers, suppliers, and the community.

**How to Spot the Clues:**

| Actions that might signal **Shareholder Primacy** | Actions that might signal **Stakeholder Primacy** |
| :--- | :--- |
| Aggressive stock buyback programs and high dividend payouts. | Reinvesting profits into employee training and benefits. |
| Layoffs and cost-cutting during periods of high profitability. | Public commitments to using ethically-sourced materials. |
| Executive compensation tied almost exclusively to stock price. | Strong investment in product safety and quality beyond regulations. |
| Lobbying against environmental or labor regulations. | Long-term partnerships with suppliers that prioritize stability over price. |
| Prioritizing speed-to-market over product quality. | Corporate philanthropy and significant community involvement. |

Your first step is to do some preliminary research and form a **working thesis**. Be open to changing your mind! The best papers often come from an analyst who changes their initial hypothesis based on the evidence they uncover.

## **:material-numeric-3-box-multiple: The Research Process**

Strong arguments are built on strong evidence. Your analysis will be far more convincing if you use credible, primary sources rather than just general news articles. Here is your research checklist:

* **The "Must-Have" - The 10-K Annual Report:** This is a company's legally required report to the government and is a treasure trove of information. You can find it on the "Investor Relations" section of any public company's website. Pay close attention to these sections:
    * `Business:` What the company says it does.
    * `Risk Factors:` What the company fears might go wrong.
    * `Management's Discussion & Analysis (MD&A):` Where leadership explains the financial results. This is often where you find the "why" behind the numbers.
* **The Proxy Statement:** This document details executive compensation and any shareholder proposals. It's the best place to find evidence of how leadership is incentivized.
* **Quarterly Earnings Call Transcripts:** Listen to how the CEO and CFO talk to Wall Street analysts. What questions do they get asked? What language do they use? Do they talk more about "maximizing shareholder value" or "long-term investment in our people"?
* **Reputable Financial Journalism:** Use sources like *The Wall Street Journal, Bloomberg, The Financial Times,* and *The Economist* for credible, in-depth analysis of your company.

## **:material-numeric-4-box-multiple: Structuring Your Analysis**

Use the following structure to organize your paper. This is not a rigid template but a logical flow to help you build a persuasive case.

1.  **Introduction:** Start with a hook that introduces your company. By the end of the introduction, you must state your **thesis** clearly. For example: *"While Johnson & Johnson publicly champions its commitment to customers and community, this paper will argue that its recent financial and operational decisions—from aggressive stock buybacks to its handling of litigation—demonstrate a firm adherence to a shareholder primacy model."*
2.  **The Company's Stated Strategy & Governance:** Briefly discuss the company's official mission statement and its strategic goals. How does the company *present* itself? Does this official story align with your thesis, or is there a contradiction you plan to explore?
3.  **Evidence from Functional Levers (The Body of Your Paper):** This is where you present your evidence. For each function, use specific examples and explain *how they support your thesis*. Ask yourself probing questions:
    * **Finance:** Does the company use its cash to reward shareholders (buybacks, dividends) or to reinvest in long-term growth (R&D, employee development)? What does this choice tell you about its priorities? How is executive pay structured?
    * **Marketing & Operations:** Does the company compete on price (suggesting cost-cutting) or on quality and brand reputation? Are there public controversies related to its supply chain ethics or product safety? How does the company's relationship with its customers support your argument?
    * **People & Talent:** What is the company's reputation as an employer? Analyze its approach to layoffs, employee benefits, training programs, and diversity and inclusion. Are employees treated as an asset to be invested in (stakeholder view) or a cost to be managed (shareholder view)?
4.  **Conclusion:** Summarize your argument in a fresh and powerful way (don't just repeat the introduction). Briefly discuss the potential long-term consequences of the company's ideological stance. What are the risks or rewards of their chosen path?

## **:material-numeric-5-box-multiple:  Writing Tips for A-Level Analysis**

The difference between a B-grade paper and an A-grade paper is the depth of analysis. Your goal is to explain the "so what?" behind every piece of evidence.

| Instead of This (Description) | Try This (Analysis) |
| :--- | :--- |
| "The company laid off 5,000 employees last year." | "The decision to lay off 5,000 employees, despite the company posting record profits that same quarter, is compelling evidence of a shareholder-first ideology. The move prioritized immediate cost savings and a boost to the stock price over the well-being and stability of its workforce." |
| "Apple spent $85 billion on stock buybacks." | "Apple's decision to allocate a staggering $85 billion to stock buybacks—an amount that could have funded groundbreaking R&D or significantly increased employee wages—serves as powerful evidence for a shareholder-centric ideology where returning capital to investors takes precedence over other strategic priorities." |

## **:material-numeric-6-box-multiple: Final Checklist**

* Is my thesis stated clearly in the introduction?
* Does every body paragraph help to support my thesis?
* Have I used specific evidence from credible sources (like the 10-K)?
* Have I drawn evidence from at least three different business functions?
* Have I focused on *analysis* (the "why" and "so what") rather than just *describing* the company's actions?
* Have I proofread the paper for clarity, grammar, and professional polish?

Good luck! I look forward to reading your analysis.
