# **A Guide to Using AI Tools Responsibly in This Course**

**Our Philosophy on AI**

Artificial intelligence tools like Gemini and NotebookLM are transforming the professional world, and learning to use them effectively, ethically, and responsibly is now a critical career skill. In this class, we will not ban these tools. Instead, we will learn to use them as what they are: incredibly powerful assistants that can augment our thinking, enhance our productivity, and deepen our learning.

This guide is designed to provide clear principles and examples for how to engage with these tools in a way that supports your learning and upholds the standards of academic integrity.

**The Core Principle**

Our entire policy can be summarized in one sentence:

!!! success "**Our Policy**"

    **The work you submit must be a genuine product of your own intellectual effort, analysis, and learning.**

AI can be a powerful partner in the process, but the final analysis, the core arguments, and the written expression must be yours. You are the analyst, the theorist, and the author; AI is your tool.

---
## **What is FAIR USE? (How to Use AI to Enhance Your Learning)**

You are **encouraged** to use AI tools in the following ways to support your work on the assignments in this course. Think of these as using a calculator for complex math or a search engine to find source material.

* **As an On-Demand Tutor:**
    * **To Clarify Concepts:** `"Explain the 'agency problem' to me. Use an analogy related to a sports team and its owner to help me understand."`
    * **To Explore Different Perspectives:** `"What are the strongest arguments *for* shareholder primacy?"`

* **As a Brainstorming Partner:**
    * **To Generate Ideas:** `"I'm interested in Nike. Help me brainstorm three different 'conceptual focus questions' that connect its marketing strategy to its corporate culture."`
    * **To Structure Your Thoughts:** `"I'm working on my Company Analysis paper. Help me create a simple outline for the 'Evidence from Functional Levers' section based on a thesis that Nike is a stakeholder-focused company."`

* **As a Research Assistant (Primarily with NotebookLM):**
    * **To Summarize Sources for Triage:** Upload a long document like a 10-K report and ask for a summary of a specific section to help you identify where to focus your own deep reading.
    * **To Find Evidence Within Your Sources:** Ask questions like, `"Based on my sources, find all mentions of 'supply chain ethics' and provide quotes with citations."`

* **As a Writing Coach:**
    * **To Improve Your Own Writing:** Paste a paragraph *you have already written* and ask, `"Can you help me make this paragraph clearer and more concise?"` or `"Can you check this text for grammatical errors?"`

---
## **What is UNACCEPTABLE USE? (Academic Misconduct)**

Submitting work that is not the product of your own intellectual effort is a serious violation of academic integrity. The following actions are considered academic misconduct in this course:

* **Plagiarism by AI:** Copying and pasting sentences, paragraphs, or entire sections of text generated by an AI model and presenting them as your own writing.
* **Misrepresentation of Research:** Using an AI to summarize an article or report and then using that summary in your work as if you have read and understood the original source yourself. Your analysis must be based on your own engagement with the materials.
* **Outsourcing Core Analysis:** Providing an AI tool with the prompt for an assignment and having it perform the central analytical task.
    * **Example of Misconduct:** `"Write an essay arguing that Tesla's management ideology is shareholder primacy, using evidence from its financial decisions and HR policies."`
* **Submitting AI-Generated Content as Your Own:** Generating any core component of an assignment (e.g., your "Analytical Narrative" or sections of your "Executive Report") and submitting it with only minor edits.

**A Simple Rule of Thumb:** Before you submit your work, ask yourself: **"Could I confidently and thoroughly explain and defend every single sentence in this submission to the professor if asked?"** If the answer is "no," you have likely crossed the line from fair use to academic misconduct.

---
## **A CRITICAL WARNING: YOU ARE RESPONSIBLE FOR YOUR WORK**

AI models are powerful, but they are not infallible. They can and do make mistakes, misunderstand context, create biased content, and "hallucinate" (make up facts and sources that seem plausible but are not true).

**You are the human analyst in charge.** If you submit a paper containing a factual error, a false source, or a flawed argument that originated from an AI tool, **you are fully responsible for that error.** You must critically evaluate, edit, and fact-check all information you use, regardless of its source.

**Citing Your Use:**
Transparency is key. At the end of any assignment where you used an AI tool for fair use purposes (like brainstorming or editing), please include a brief "Process Note" explaining how you used it.

* *Example Process Note:* "I used Gemini to brainstorm initial ideas for my conceptual focus question. I also used it to check my final draft of the Theoretical Narrative for grammatical errors."

This course is an opportunity to develop cutting-edge skills. Let's approach these new tools with curiosity, integrity, and a commitment to producing our best, most authentic work.
