# Lesson Content: Concept Mapping Session

This issue tracks the creation of the lesson content for the session on concept mapping. The content should be comprehensive and guide students through understanding and creating their own concept maps.

## Content Sections

The final lesson plan should include the following sections:

### 1. The Big Picture
- [ ] Explain the purpose of concept mapping in the context of this course.
- [ ] Connect concept mapping to the broader goal of developing an integrated theory of management.
- [ ] Briefly introduce the final deliverable (Concept Map & Theoretical Narrative).

### 2. Anatomy of a Concept Map
- [ ] Define the core components: Concepts, Hierarchy, and Cross-links.
- [ ] Provide a clear visual example of a simple concept map.
- [ ] Explain the difference between hierarchical links and analytical cross-links.
- [ ] Discuss the importance of labeling cross-links with explanatory phrases.

### 3. Guided Activity
- [ ] Design a short, in-class activity to be done collaboratively.
- [ ] Provide a small set of core concepts for students to work with.
- [ ] The goal is to practice creating a small map and identifying potential cross-links as a group.

### 4. Independent Activity
- [ ] Design an activity for students to start working on their own project.
- [ ] Task students with developing their initial "conceptual focus question".
- [ ] Guide them in identifying their "Tier 1" concepts based on their question.
- [ ] Students should create a preliminary, rough draft of their concept map.

### 5. Next Steps
- [ ] Outline the homework and next steps following the session.
- [ ] Remind students to continue refining their focus question and map.
- [ ] Point them to the full `concept-map-guide.md` for detailed instructions.
